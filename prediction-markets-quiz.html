Prediction Markets: The Future of Event Betting and Alternative Investing

Reading time: 6 minutes | Quiz: 10 questions

Advertisement

Imagine being able to bet on whether it will rain tomorrow, who will win the next presidential election, or if the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates—and doing it legally, through regulated platforms. Welcome to the world of prediction markets, where the wisdom of crowds meets financial speculation. In 2024 and 2025, these markets exploded into the mainstream, with platforms like Kalshi winning legal battles and Polymarket processing billions in trading volume.

Prediction markets are exchanges where participants trade contracts whose payoff depends on the outcome of future events. Unlike traditional betting, these markets are structured as financial instruments—buying "yes" shares at $0.60 means you'll profit if the event happens (paying out $1) or lose your investment if it doesn't. This structure attracts a different crowd than typical sportsbooks: data analysts, political junkies, and investors looking for uncorrelated returns.

Kalshi: The Regulated Pioneer

Kalshi made history by becoming the first legal, regulated prediction market in the United States. After a years-long battle with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), Kalshi won the right to offer event contracts on political outcomes, economic indicators, and cultural events. Unlike offshore betting sites, Kalshi operates under federal oversight, meaning your funds are protected and the platform follows strict financial regulations.

On Kalshi, you can trade on questions like "Will the S&P 500 end the year up?" or "Will Taylor Swift's next album drop in Q2?" The prices reflect the market's collective belief about probability—if "yes" shares trade at $0.75, the market believes there's a 75% chance of that outcome occurring. This creates fascinating opportunities for informed traders who believe they have better information than the crowd.

Polymarket: The Crypto Alternative

While Kalshi operates within US regulations, Polymarket took a different approach—building on blockchain technology and operating internationally. Polymarket exploded in popularity during the 2024 election cycle, with billions in trading volume on political outcomes. The platform uses cryptocurrency for deposits and withdrawals, allowing users worldwide to participate (where legally permitted).

Polymarket's liquidity and user base make it particularly attractive for high-volume traders. During major events like election nights, millions of dollars flow through the platform as traders react to breaking news in real-time. The platform's "order book" structure means you're trading directly with other users rather than against a house, potentially offering better odds for informed bettors.

Prediction Markets as Insurance

One of the most interesting applications of prediction markets is hedging—using them as insurance against real-world outcomes. Imagine you work in a industry heavily affected by election outcomes. You could buy shares predicting the candidate whose victory would hurt your industry. If they win, your investment profits offset your professional losses. If they lose, you're happy with the election result and your shares expire worthless—a small price for peace of mind.

Companies are also exploring prediction markets for forecasting. Instead of relying on internal forecasts, businesses can look at prediction market prices to gauge probabilities of regulatory changes, economic shifts, or competitor moves. The "wisdom of crowds" often outperforms individual experts, making these markets valuable tools for decision-making.

Risks and Realities

Despite the excitement, prediction markets carry significant risks. They're still gambling—most participants lose money. The markets can be manipulated, liquidity can dry up, and unexpected events can render even "sure bets" worthless. Additionally, the regulatory landscape remains uncertain, particularly for crypto-based platforms like Polymarket.
