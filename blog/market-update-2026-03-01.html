Market Update: Nvidia Smashes Earnings, Bitcoin Bounces Back, Fed Holds Steady

📅 March 1, 2026 • ⏱️ 7 min read

GG, money gamers! This week was like clutching a 1v5 in ranked — just when things looked bleak, Nvidia dropped a nuke of an earnings report and the markets found their footing. We've got the S&P 500 posting back-to-back gains, Bitcoin staging a comeback, and enough Big Tech drama to fill a Twitch stream. Let's dive into the highlights.

📈 The Market Snapshot

The S&P 500 just notched back-to-back daily gains for the first time in what feels like forever, closing Thursday at 6,946.13 — up 0.81% on the day. The Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.26% to 23,152.08, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 307 points (0.8%).

After a brutal February that saw the Nasdaq post its longest weekly losing streak since May 2022, this bounce feels like finally getting that win after a tilted losing streak. But is it a true comeback or just a dead cat bounce? Let's look at the evidence.

🚀 Nvidia: The AI King Delivers

All eyes were on Nvidia (NVDA) this week, and Jensen Huang did NOT disappoint. The company reported record Q4 and fiscal 2026 results that had Wall Street doing victory laps:

  • Revenue: $68.1 billion for Q4 (up 20% from last quarter)
  • Data Center Revenue: Up a mind-blowing 75% year-over-year
  • EPS: $1.62 adjusted vs. $1.53 estimated — a clean beat
  • Full Year 2026: Record-breaking performance across the board

The AI boom is very much alive, and Nvidia is still the primary beneficiary. Data center spending from hyperscalers like Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta continues to pour in as they race to build out AI infrastructure. It's like everyone decided to buy the best GPU on the market at the same time — and Nvidia is the only shop in town.

🎯 The AI Trade Lives On

Nvidia's blowout quarter validates the massive capex spending from Big Tech. Yes, investors were worried about returns — but when the company supplying the picks and shovels to the AI gold rush is printing $68B quarters, maybe those concerns were overblown.

That said, there are still questions. Nvidia disclosed that two customers made up 36% of sales — that's concentration risk that keeps some investors up at night. If Microsoft or Meta suddenly slam the brakes on AI spending, Nvidia feels it immediately.

📱 Big Tech Check-In

Here's how the rest of the Magnificent 7 are looking after this week's action:

  • Meta (META): Still riding high after crushing earnings last month — the social media pivot to AI is paying off
  • Microsoft (MSFT): Trading around $401, still down ~27% from highs but showing signs of stabilization
  • Amazon (AMZN): Around $210, recovering from bear market territory as investors digest that massive $200B capex plan
  • Apple (AAPL): Steady at ~$264 — not exciting, but not falling apart either
  • Tesla (TSLA): Hovering around $402-$411 range, up 2.4% on recent trading sessions but still down YTD
  • Alphabet (GOOGL): Trading around $312, trying to find support after a rough month

The narrative is shifting from "AI bubble bursting" to "AI infrastructure buildout continuing." Nvidia's earnings gave everyone permission to believe again — at least for now.

₿ Crypto: Bitcoin's Bounce Play

Crypto holders can breathe a small sigh of relief this week. After dipping as low as $60,000 and sparking panic about a potential breakdown, Bitcoin staged a meaningful recovery:

  • Bitcoin (BTC): Bounced roughly 9% from Tuesday's lows, briefly touching $70,000 before settling around $67,000
  • Ethereum (ETH): Trading around $2,047-$2,027, mirroring Bitcoin's moves but still stuck in a range
  • Altcoins: Mixed bag — some AI-linked tokens are advancing while majors follow BTC's lead

The technical picture is interesting here. Bitcoin recovered from "deeply oversold conditions" and managed to reclaim the $67K support level. But don't get too bullish yet — the price action is still range-bound, and retail demand has been deteriorating according to on-chain data.

⚠️ Reality Check: Bitcoin's "safe-haven" status has been questioned lately, with some analysts arguing it's behaving more like a risk asset than digital gold. The correlation with tech stocks has been uncomfortably high during this volatility.

Crypto Regulatory Front

On the regulatory side, there's some positive momentum:

  • SEC streamlined listing standards are making it easier for new crypto ETFs to launch
  • South Korea's FSS released a 2026 policy roadmap signaling tighter supervision but clearer rules
  • XRP is surging toward key resistance on regulatory optimism and ETF speculation

The regulatory environment is slowly shifting from "hostile" to "structured" — which is better than nothing, even if it's not the Wild West freedom some crypto purists want.

🏛️ Macro: Fed Holds the Line

The Federal Reserve isn't riding to the rescue anytime soon. Recent Fed minutes confirmed what we already suspected: the pause on rate cuts continues.

Key takeaways from the Fed's February meeting:

  • Inflation data needs to cooperate before any more cuts
  • The Fed is watching economic growth indicators closely
  • Market expectations have shifted to maybe 1-2 cuts in 2026 (down from 3-4 expected earlier)

Current interest rates are holding steady around 3.60-3.67% on the short end. For investors, this means the "easy money" environment of 2024 isn't coming back immediately. Companies actually have to earn their valuations now — imagine that.

👀 What to Watch Next Week

🎯 Key Events on the Radar

March 3-7 Trading Sessions: All eyes will be on whether this week's bounce has legs. Watch for follow-through buying or another round of profit-taking.

  • Bitcoin $70K Resistance: Can BTC break and hold above $70K? That's the level that separates "recovery" from "rally"
  • Tech Stock Momentum: Nvidia's earnings gave the sector a shot in the arm — watch if other names follow through or if it's just an NVDA-specific move
  • Economic Data: Manufacturing PMI and jobs data could move the needle on Fed expectations
  • Tariff Developments: The trade war saga continues — any new executive orders or retaliatory measures could spark volatility
  • Retail Investor Flows: VandaTrack showed weak retail participation recently. If the little guys start buying again, it could fuel a sustained move higher

🎮 The Bottom Line

This week felt like the market finally found its respawn point after a brutal February. Nvidia's earnings proved that the AI trade still has juice, Bitcoin bounced off scary lows, and the S&P 500 managed two green days in a row (a low bar, but we'll take it).

For investors: The dip-buying opportunity in quality tech names might be closing. Amazon and Microsoft still look interesting if you believe in the long-term AI infrastructure buildout. But don't FOMO — there will be other chances.

For crypto holders: The $60K-$65K range is holding as support for now, which is encouraging. But until Bitcoin can reclaim and hold $70K with conviction, this is just a bounce within a range, not the start of a new bull run.

For everyone: Cash still yields 4%+ in high-yield savings, and that's not nothing in a volatile market. There's no shame in sitting on the sidelines while the macro picture sorts itself out.

Remember: markets are a marathon, not a sprint. This week's wins feel good, but the game isn't over. Stay sharp, manage your risk, and don't bet the farm on any single trade. 🎮
