How to Start an At-Home Business: Complete 2026 Guide

February 21, 2026 • 15 min read • Step-by-Step Guide
C
Credit Gamer Area
Business & Finance Writer

Starting a business from home has never been more accessible. Whether you're selling products on Etsy, offering consulting services, or building the next great app, you can launch with minimal upfront investment. But doing it legally and smartly requires following specific steps.

This guide walks you through everything — from choosing your business structure to filing taxes — so you can start your at-home business with confidence.

💡 Quick Overview: What You'll Learn

By the end of this guide, you'll know how to: choose a business structure, form an LLC, get an EIN, open a business bank account, set up accounting, understand taxes, and ensure you're legally compliant — all from your home office.

1

Choose Your Business Structure

Before you register anything, decide how your business will be legally structured. This affects your taxes, liability, and paperwork requirements.

Sole Proprietorship

Best for: Side hustles, testing business ideas, very low risk

  • ✅ Easiest to set up (no formal registration needed)
  • ✅ No separate business tax return
  • ❌ Personal liability: Your personal assets are at risk if sued
  • ❌ Harder to get business credit

Limited Liability Company (LLC)

Best for: Most small businesses, protecting personal assets

  • ✅ Personal asset protection (liability shield)
  • ✅ Tax flexibility (can choose how you're taxed)
  • ✅ More professional credibility
  • ⚠️ Costs $50-500 to form (varies by state)
  • ⚠️ Annual report fees in some states

S Corporation (S-Corp)

Best for: Profitable businesses ($40K+ net income) wanting tax savings

  • ✅ Potential tax savings on self-employment tax
  • ❌ More complex setup and ongoing requirements
  • ❌ Must pay yourself a "reasonable salary"

⚠️ Our Recommendation

For most at-home businesses, start with an LLC. It gives you liability protection without the complexity of a corporation. You can always elect S-Corp tax status later once profitable.

2

Form Your LLC (If Applicable)

If you chose LLC (recommended), here's how to form it:

Option A: Do It Yourself (Cheapest)

  1. Choose your state: Usually your home state. Don't fall for "form in Delaware/Wyoming" unless you have specific reasons.
  2. Check name availability: Search your state's business entity database to ensure your desired name isn't taken.
  3. File Articles of Organization: Submit to your state's Secretary of State office. Most allow online filing.
  4. Pay filing fee: $50-$500 depending on state (see table below).
  5. Create an Operating Agreement: Not always required, but highly recommended. Templates available online.

Option B: Use a Formation Service (Easiest)

Services like ZenBusiness, Northwest Registered Agent, or LegalZoom handle everything for $100-300 plus state fees.

3

Get an EIN (Employer Identification Number)

An EIN is like a Social Security number for your business. You need it to:

  • Open a business bank account
  • File business taxes
  • Hire employees (if applicable)
  • Build business credit

How to Get Your EIN (Free)

  1. Go to irs.gov and search "EIN application"
  2. Click "Apply for an EIN Online"
  3. Complete the application (takes 10-15 minutes)
  4. Receive your EIN immediately upon completion

⚠️ Important Notes

  • The EIN application is FREE — never pay a third party for this
  • You can only apply online Monday-Friday, 7am-10pm ET
  • Sole proprietors without employees can use their SSN, but an EIN is still recommended for privacy
4

Open a Business Bank Account

Never mix personal and business finances. This is crucial for maintaining your LLC's liability protection and makes tax time much easier.

What You'll Need

  • EIN (from Step 3)
  • LLC formation documents (Articles of Organization)
  • Operating Agreement (some banks require this)
  • Personal ID (driver's license/passport)
  • Initial deposit (varies by bank, often $0-100)

6

Understand Your Tax Obligations

Taxes are where many new business owners get caught off guard. Here's what you need to know:

Types of Taxes You May Owe

  • Income Tax: Federal and state taxes on your business profit
  • Self-Employment Tax: 15.3% for Social Security and Medicare (you pay both employer and employee portions)
  • Sales Tax: If you sell physical products or certain services (varies by state)
  • Quarterly Estimated Taxes: Pay-as-you-go system for income and self-employment tax

Quarterly Estimated Tax Deadlines (2026)

  • Q1 (Jan-Mar): April 15, 2026
  • Q2 (Apr-May): June 15, 2026
  • Q3 (Jun-Aug): September 15, 2026
  • Q4 (Sep-Dec): January 15, 2027

💡 Tax Savings Tips

  • Set aside 25-30% of profit for taxes in a separate savings account
  • Deduct your home office (if exclusively used for business)
  • Track and deduct business mileage
  • Deduct health insurance premiums (if self-employed)
  • Consider hiring a CPA — often pays for itself in tax savings
7

Get Required Licenses and Permits

Depending on your business type and location, you may need:

Common Requirements

  • Business License: General license to operate in your city/county
  • Home Occupation Permit: If running a business from home (check local zoning laws)
  • Seller's Permit: Required to collect sales tax (if selling products)
  • Professional Licenses: For specific professions (real estate, cosmetology, etc.)
  • Food Handler's License: If selling food products

⚠️ Check Your Local Requirements

Every city and state has different requirements. Contact your local Small Business Development Center (SBDC) or city clerk's office to find out exactly what you need. Many requirements can be researched online at your state's business portal.

8

Set Up Your Home Office (Legally)

If you're working from home, there are legal and tax considerations:

Zoning and HOA Rules

  • Check if your neighborhood allows home businesses
  • Review HOA rules (if applicable)
  • Some areas prohibit customer traffic or signage
  • Most residential areas allow "low-impact" home offices

Home Office Tax Deduction

You can deduct home office expenses if:

  • The space is used exclusively for business
  • It's your principal place of business
  • You can deduct a percentage of rent/mortgage, utilities, insurance, and repairs

💡 Simplified Option

The IRS offers a simplified deduction: $5 per square foot of home office space (up to 300 sq ft = $1,500 max deduction). This is easier than tracking actual expenses but may give you a smaller deduction.

9

⚠️ Don't Rely Only on LLC Protection

An LLC protects your personal assets from business debts, but it doesn't protect your business from lawsuits. Insurance covers legal defense costs and settlements that could otherwise bankrupt your company.

10

Build Your Brand and Online Presence

Now for the fun part — creating your brand and getting customers!

Essential Branding Steps

  • Domain name: Secure your business name as a .com (use Namecheap, Google Domains, or Cloudflare)
  • Website: Build a simple site (Squarespace, Wix, WordPress, or Carrd for landing pages)
  • Professional email: yourname@yourbusiness.com (Google Workspace ~$6/month)
  • Social media: Secure handles on platforms where your customers are
  • Logo and branding: Canva (DIY) or 99designs/Fiverr (professional)

Marketing on a Budget

  • Content marketing: Start a blog or YouTube channel
  • Social media: Organic posting on Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn
  • Email marketing: Build a list from day one (ConvertKit, Mailchimp)
  • Networking: Join Facebook groups, Reddit communities, local business groups
  • Referrals: Ask happy customers for referrals and reviews

💡 Remember

Many successful businesses started with less than $1,000. Don't let costs stop you — start lean, validate your business idea, and reinvest profits as you grow.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not separating finances: Mixing personal and business money creates tax nightmares and pierces your LLC protection
  • Ignoring quarterly taxes: That year-end tax bill can be shocking if you haven't been setting money aside
  • Skipping contracts: Always use contracts with clients, even friends and family
  • No business insurance: One lawsuit without insurance can end your business
  • Perfectionism: Don't wait for everything to be perfect — launch and iterate
  • Doing everything yourself: Know when to hire professionals (CPA, lawyer, designer)
