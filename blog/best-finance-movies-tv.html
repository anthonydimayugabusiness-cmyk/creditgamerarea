12 Best Finance Movies & TV Shows

Learn about money while being entertained

I learned more about the 2008 financial crisis from The Big Short than from any textbook. These movies and shows make complex financial concepts accessible while telling incredible stories. Perfect for date night or binge-watching.

🎬 Must-Watch Movies

1. The Big Short (2015)

Christian Bale, Steve Carell, Ryan Gosling | 2h 10m
⭐ 8.5/10 - Explains the 2008 crisis perfectly
Available on: Netflix, Amazon Prime, Apple TV

The movie that made mortgage-backed securities entertaining. Follows the few investors who saw the 2008 housing collapse coming and bet against the market. The celebrity cameos explaining complex terms (Margot Robbie in a bathtub) are genius.

What you'll learn: CDOs, subprime mortgages, credit default swaps, and how Wall Street almost destroyed the economy.

View on IMDb →

2. The Wolf of Wall Street (2013)

Leonardo DiCaprio, Jonah Hill | 3h
⭐ 8.2/10 - Cautionary tale about greed
Available on: Paramount+, Amazon Prime, Apple TV

Martin Scorsese's wild ride through Jordan Belfort's fraudulent stock brokerage. Yes, it's excessive and debaucherous - that's the point. Shows how pump-and-dump schemes work and why regulation exists.

What you'll learn: Penny stocks, pump-and-dump schemes, SEC investigations, and why "too good to be true" usually is.

View on IMDb →

3. Margin Call (2011)

Kevin Spacey, Jeremy Irons, Zachary Quinto | 1h 47m
⭐ 7.1/10 - The 24 hours before the crash
Available on: Amazon Prime, Apple TV, Hulu

A fictional investment bank discovers they're bankrupt - over one night. More realistic than The Big Short. Shows the human cost of financial decisions made at the top.

What you'll learn: How investment banks work, risk management failures, and why 2008 happened.

View on IMDb →

4. Wall Street (1987)

Michael Douglas, Charlie Sheen | 2h 6m
⭐ 7.3/10 - "Greed is good"
Available on: Hulu, Amazon Prime, Apple TV

The original finance movie. Gordon Gekko's "Greed is good" speech defined 1980s excess. Follows a young stockbroker who gets seduced by insider trading. Still relevant today.

What you'll learn: Insider trading, 1980s corporate culture, and why ethics matter in finance.

View on IMDb →

5. Boiler Room (2000)

Giovanni Ribisi, Vin Diesel, Ben Affleck | 2h
⭐ 7.0/10 - Pump and dump schemes
Available on: Amazon Prime, Apple TV, Tubi

A college dropout joins a shady brokerage and gets rich selling worthless stocks. Based on real "chop shops" that defrauded investors. The "Always Be Closing" energy but for stocks.

What you'll learn: How scam brokerages operate, cold calling tactics, and red flags to watch for.

View on IMDb →

6. Inside Job (2010)

Documentary narrated by Matt Damon | 1h 49m
⭐ 8.2/10 - The definitive 2008 documentary
Available on: Amazon Prime, Apple TV, YouTube

The documentary that explains exactly what caused the 2008 financial crisis - and who was responsible. Won the Oscar for Best Documentary. Essential viewing for understanding modern finance.

What you'll learn: The complete story of 2008, from deregulation to the bailout.

View on IMDb →

📺 Best TV Shows

7. Billions (2016-2023)

Paul Giamatti, Damian Lewis | 7 Seasons
⭐ 8.4/10 - Hedge fund drama
Available on: Paramount+, Showtime, Amazon Prime

The cat-and-mouse game between a hedge fund billionaire and a US Attorney. Explores insider trading, short squeezes, and the gray areas of finance. The writing is sharp and the finance is surprisingly accurate.

What you'll learn: Hedge funds, insider trading investigations, and how the ultra-wealthy play by different rules.

View on IMDb →

8. Industry (2020-)

Myha'la Herrold, Marisa Abela | 3 Seasons
⭐ 7.2/10 - Investment banking from the bottom
Available on: HBO Max, Amazon Prime

Follows young graduates at a prestigious London investment bank. Shows the brutal reality of finance careers - the hours, the pressure, the money. Like "Succession" meets "Euphoria" but for banking.

What you'll learn: What investment bankers actually do, FX trading, and the cost of ambition.

View on IMDb →

9. Bad Blood (Documentary Series)

The Theranos scandal | Multiple series available
⭐ Various - Startup fraud exposed
Available on: Hulu (The Dropout), HBO Max, Netflix

The story of Elizabeth Holmes and Theranos - how a $9 billion startup was built on lies. Multiple documentaries and the drama series "The Dropout" with Amanda Seyfried. Fascinating look at startup culture and fraud.

What you'll learn: Startup valuations, venture capital, due diligence, and red flags in "unicorn" companies.

View The Dropout on IMDb →

10. WeCrashed (2022)

Jared Leto, Anne Hathaway | 8 Episodes
⭐ 7.3/10 - The WeWork disaster
Available on: Apple TV+

The rise and fall of WeWork and its eccentric founder Adam Neumann. Shows how hype, personality cults, and SoftBank money created a $47 billion valuation for a company that was essentially subleasing office space.

What you'll learn: Startup valuations, venture capital bubbles, and why "vision" doesn't pay the bills.

View on IMDb →

11. Super Pumped (2022-2023)

Joseph Gordon-Levitt | 2 Seasons (Uber & Airbnb)
⭐ 7.4/10 - Startup founder psychology
Available on: Showtime, Paramount+

Season 1 covers Travis Kalanick and Uber's toxic rise. Season 2 covers Brian Chesky and Airbnb. Both show how "move fast and break things" culture led to massive success and massive problems.

What you'll learn: Startup funding rounds, founder psychology, and the cost of growth at all costs.

View on IMDb →

12. The Smart Money Woman (2020)

Nigerian series | 7 Episodes
⭐ 6.8/10 - African finance drama
Available on: Netflix

A Nigerian series about a group of friends navigating careers, relationships, and money in Lagos. Based on the book by Arese Ugwu. Refreshing perspective on finance outside the US/Europe bubble.

What you'll learn: Personal finance is universal - debt, investing, and relationships with money are global themes.

View on IMDb →

My Watch Order Recommendation

If you're new to finance movies, watch in this order:

  1. The Big Short - Entertaining intro to modern finance
  2. Inside Job - The documentary reality check
  3. Margin Call - See the human side of the crisis
  4. Wall Street - Understand the history
  5. Billions - Deep dive into hedge funds (TV series)

Date night idea: Pick one movie, then discuss what you learned over dinner. It's actually a great way to talk about money with your partner without it being awkward.
