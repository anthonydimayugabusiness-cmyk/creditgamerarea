Market Update: S&P 500 Bounces Back, Nvidia Earnings Loom, Crypto Chills at $67K

February 20, 2026 • 7 min read

What's poppin', traders? Another week, another rollercoaster in the markets. If you've been busy grinding your battle pass or catching up on sleep, don't sweat it—I got the full recap for you. Here's everything that went down from Tuesday through Friday this week.

Spoiler alert: The S&P 500 is trying to claw its way back, Big Tech is sending mixed signals, and crypto is just vibing in a tight range. Let's break it down.

📊 The Big Picture: S&P 500 Finds Its Footing

After getting rejected at 7,000 last week like a bad pickup line, the S&P 500 decided to stop the bleeding. We're sitting around 6,876 as of Friday, up about 0.20% from the previous session. It's not a moon shot, but hey—green is green.

The Nasdaq managed to gain some ground too, climbing roughly 0.9% earlier in the week before giving back some gains. The Dow? It's been chopping around, down about 0.54% as traders digest mixed economic signals.

What's driving this? A combo of oversold bounces, some decent economic data, and traders positioning ahead of Nvidia's massive earnings report next week. The market's been weird lately—stocks swinging like there's a crisis, but the S&P 500 is only about 2% from its highs. Classic "consolidation with anxiety" vibes.

💻 Big Tech: The Magnificent Seven Send Mixed Signals

The Mag 7 is acting like a dysfunctional squad in ranked—some are carrying, others are feeding hard. Here's the scoreboard:

NVDA Nvidia

Trading around $188, up from the mid-$180s earlier this week. All eyes are on their earnings report dropping February 25. Analysts expect EPS of $1.52 on revenue of $65.6 billion—that's 71% year-over-year growth, which is absolutely bonkers. But here's the thing: expectations are sky-high, and some analysts are predicting a drop after earnings regardless of the beat. The stock's up huge over the past two years (from ~$68 in Feb 2024), so profit-taking pressure is real. Will Jensen Huang deliver the goods? Place your bets.

TSLA Tesla

Hovering around $411, basically flat for the week. Option traders are showing bearish sentiment, with puts getting more action than calls. The Cybertruck hype has faded, competition in China is heating up, and Elon's been... well, Elon. Robotaxis are still "coming soon" (where have we heard that before?). At a $1.4T market cap, there's a lot of growth priced in. Can they deliver?

META Meta

The standout performer of the group. After crushing Q4 earnings with EPS of $8.88 (vs. $8.23 expected), the stock climbed nearly 10%. Zuckerberg's planning to spend $115-135 billion on AI infrastructure in 2026, and Wall Street is actually cheering the capex this time. Revenue guidance implies 20%+ growth ahead. Sometimes spending big works—who knew?

MSFT Microsoft

Still stuck in the mud, down about 16% YTD. Azure growth is slowing, and investors are questioning whether that massive AI spend is paying off. Amazon and Microsoft both announced huge capex numbers ($200B for AMZN, massive AI buildout for MSFT), and the market's response has been... skeptical. The AI trade is getting a reality check.

The takeaway? Not all Big Tech is created equal right now. Meta's execution is winning praise, while Microsoft and Amazon are getting side-eyed for their spending. Nvidia's earnings next week could decide whether the AI trade gets a second wind or continues to bleed out.

₿ Crypto Corner: Bitcoin Chills, Ethereum Snoozes

While stocks were doing their chaotic dance, crypto was surprisingly zen. Here's the TL;DR:

  • Bitcoin (BTC): Trading in the $67,000-$68,000 range, showing low volatility after that wild Feb 5 selloff. The Fear & Greed index is sitting at 10/100—extreme fear territory—which historically has been a contrarian buy signal. But don't quote me on that.
  • Ethereum (ETH): Hovering near $1,970-$2,050, lagging Bitcoin as usual. Some analysts see $2,100-$2,300 as key support, with $2,500-$2,700 as resistance. ETH has been the underperforming little brother lately, down about 60% from its all-time highs vs. Bitcoin's 47% drawdown.

🔥 Crypto News That Actually Matters

Harvard's Crypto Strategy Shift: In a plot twist, Harvard's endowment sold some Bitcoin positions but increased their Ethereum holdings. When Ivy League endowments are rebalancing crypto portfolios, you know this asset class has gone mainstream. The smart money is still playing the long game.

Trump Media Files Crypto ETFs: DJT (Trump Media) filed for two new crypto ETF proposals after the SEC delayed their previous application. Love him or hate him, Trump's crypto agenda is keeping the regulatory conversation alive. More ETF products = more institutional access = potentially more flows.

UK Reverses Retail Crypto Ban: Across the pond, the UK lifted its ban on retail crypto ETFs. This follows a trend of regulatory softening globally. The UK FCA (their version of the SEC) is basically saying "fine, you can play, but don't come crying to us when you get rekt." Progress?

🎮 Gamer Take

Bitcoin holding steady at $67K despite all the macro noise is actually bullish. It means there's real demand underneath—institutions, ETFs, and hodlers aren't panic-selling. That said, crypto can still nuke 20% overnight because some whale sneezed or Elon tweets something weird. If you're DCAing, keep at it. If you're trading, watch that $65K support level like your ranked LP.

🌍 Macro Watch: Fed Minutes, Economic Data, and Geopolitics

Here's what's moving the macro needle this week:

  • Fed Minutes Drop: The Fed released their January meeting minutes, and the vibe was "pause and assess." Inflation is still sticky, and they're in no rush to cut rates. The market wanted dovish signals. The Fed said "nah." Classic standoff continues.
  • Strong Manufacturing PMI: US manufacturing came in hot, which is good for the economy but bad for rate cut hopes. The Fed watches this stuff closely. Strong economy = less urgency to ease policy.
  • Geopolitical Tensions: Sanctions on Russia are tightening, and tensions in the Middle East keep oil prices elevated. Not great for risk assets, but the market's been shrugging it off so far.
  • UK Inflation Data: British inflation numbers came in higher than expected, which is putting pressure on the Bank of England to keep rates higher for longer. Global central banks are mostly singing the same song: "inflation isn't dead yet."

The Fed Funds rate is still sitting at 6.75% (prime rate), and futures are pricing in a "gradual decline" rather than aggressive cuts. Translation: don't expect cheap money anytime soon.

👀 What to Watch Next Week

  • Nvidia Earnings (Feb 25): This is the main event. The AI trade lives or dies by this report. Revenue of $65.6B expected. Anything less than spectacular could trigger a tech selloff. No pressure, Jensen.
  • More Fed Speak: Fed officials are hitting the speech circuit. Any hints about rate cut timing will move markets instantly. Watch for the word "patient"—that's Fed-speak for "not cutting yet."
  • S&P 500 at 7,000: Can we finally break and hold this level? Or are we looking at more chop? The next few sessions will be telling. A close above 7K would be bullish. Failure here could mean a deeper pullback.
  • Crypto ETF Flows: Keep an eye on sustained inflows into Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs. That's your signal that institutional adoption is real, not just hype. Outflows = bad vibes.
  • Economic Data: Jobless claims, PMI data, and consumer sentiment numbers are all on the calendar. Strong data = good for stocks short-term, bad for rate cut hopes.

🎯 Final Boss Thoughts

The market's in a weird consolidation phase right now. We're not crashing, but we're not ripping higher either. The S&P 500 is stuck in a range, Big Tech is showing divergent fortunes, and crypto is waiting for its next catalyst.

For the 18-30 crowd: This is actually a decent time to be patient. If you've been DCAing into index funds, keep doing your thing—choppy markets reward consistency. If you're picking individual stocks, be selective. Quality matters when valuations are stretched and volatility is elevated.

And if you're in crypto? Don't panic over the sideways action. Bitcoin holding $67K despite all the macro uncertainty is quietly bullish. The next leg up (or down) will likely come from either a major regulatory development or a shift in Fed policy. Until then, accumulate if you believe in the long-term thesis, but never risk more than you can afford to lose completely.

The game isn't over. It's just in a loading screen. Time to review your strategy and prep for the next level.

Stay liquid, stay informed, and may your portfolios be ever green. 🎮📈
