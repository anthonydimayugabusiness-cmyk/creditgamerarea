The Ultimate Guide to Round-the-World Tickets on Miles: Fly Around the Globe for Less

How to book epic multi-continent journeys using airline miles, maximize stopovers, and visit 5+ countries for the price of one business class ticket

📅 Feb 23, 2026 ⏱️ 15 min read ✈️ Award Travel Mastery

Imagine boarding a plane in New York, waking up in Tokyo, spending a week exploring ancient temples in Bangkok, sipping wine in Paris, and returning home — all for fewer miles than a simple round-trip to Europe would cost.

This isn't a travel agent's fantasy. This is the power of Round-the-World (RTW) award tickets — one of the best-kept secrets in the points and miles world.

For years, savvy travelers have been leveraging airline alliance programs to book incredible multi-stop journeys that would cost $10,000+ in cash for a fraction of the miles. In this comprehensive guide, we'll break down everything you need to know: the best programs, routing rules, booking strategies, and real-world examples you can book today.

What Is a Round-the-World Ticket?

A Round-the-World (RTW) ticket is a single award booking that takes you around the globe, typically allowing 3 to 15 stops across multiple continents. Unlike regular award tickets that limit you to simple round-trips or one-way flights, RTW awards are designed for travelers who want to see the world without breaking the bank.

The key benefits:

  • Incredible value: Visit 5-8 cities for the same miles as a standard business class ticket
  • Multiple stopovers: Stay days or weeks in each destination without extra cost
  • Open jaws: Fly into one city and out of another, exploring overland
  • One booking, one price: Simplify complex multi-city itineraries
"I booked a 6-continent, 35,000-mile journey visiting 12 cities over 6 weeks. Cash price: $18,500. Miles cost: 125,000 ANA miles plus $800 in taxes. That's a value of over 14 cents per mile." — Award Travel Enthusiast, FlyerTalk Forums

Understanding RTW Routing Rules

Each program has specific rules that determine what constitutes a valid RTW itinerary. Violate these rules, and the airline won't ticket your award. Here are the key concepts:

📋 Essential RTW Routing Rules

  • Direction of travel: Most programs require you to travel consistently eastbound OR westbound — no backtracking across oceans
  • Ocean crossings: You must cross both the Atlantic and Pacific oceans (or vice versa) to qualify as RTW
  • Maximum mileage: Some programs limit total distance (e.g., 35,000 miles for ANA)
  • Maximum segments: Limits on total flight segments (usually 8-16)
  • Stopover limits: Each program defines how many free stopovers you can include
  • Surface segments: Ground transportation between cities counts as a segment but not mileage
  • Origin/destination: Most require you to return to your origin country (not necessarily the same city)

ANA Mileage Club: The Gold Standard

ANA offers the best RTW value in the industry, but their rules are strict. Here's what you need to know:

  • Seasonal pricing: Low season (125K business), Regular (145K), High (170K)
  • Direction: Must travel consistently eastbound OR westbound
  • Ocean crossings: Must cross both Atlantic and Pacific exactly once each
  • Maximum distance: 22,000-29,000 miles depending on award type
  • Stopovers: Up to 8 stopovers, with up to 4 in Europe
  • Segments: Maximum 12 flight segments
  • Booking: Must call ANA; cannot book online

Air Canada Aeroplan: Maximum Flexibility

Aeroplan doesn't have a specific RTW award, but their multi-city tool effectively allows RTW bookings:

  • Pricing: Distance-based chart (0-4,000 miles, 4,001-6,000, etc.)
  • Direction: No restrictions — you can backtrack within regions
  • Stopovers: One free stopover or open jaw on one-way awards; up to 6 one-ways on a single ticket
  • Segments: Up to 6 flight segments per one-way award
  • Booking: Fully bookable online — the easiest RTW option

Maximizing Stopovers: The Secret to Amazing RTW Trips

The real magic of RTW tickets lies in stopovers — the ability to spend days or weeks in a city without using additional miles. Here's how to maximize them:

🗾

Asia Stopover Strategy

Use Tokyo (ANA/JAL hub), Seoul (Korean Air), or Singapore as gateways. Add extended stops in Bangkok, Bali, or Hong Kong for minimal extra cost.

🏛️

Europe Stopover Strategy

Most programs allow 2-4 European stops. Consider Istanbul (Turkish hub), Frankfurt (Lufthansa), or Zurich (Swiss) as connection points with free stopovers.

🌴

South Pacific Strategy

Include Auckland or Sydney as stopovers. Many RTW routings naturally pass through these cities — turn a connection into a week-long adventure.

🌉

North America Strategy

Use your home city as the turnaround point. Add stopovers in Honolulu, Vancouver, or your departure gateway for bonus destinations.

Stopover vs. Connection: Know the Difference

A connection is typically under 24 hours (or 4 hours domestically). A stopover is longer than 24 hours and counts toward your stopover limit. Some programs also allow open jaws — flying into one city and out of another — which don't count as stopovers but let you explore overland.

How to Book Your RTW Ticket: Step-by-Step

🚀 Your RTW Booking Checklist

  1. Choose Your Program — ANA for best value, Aeroplan for flexibility, or another program based on your points balances and desired airlines.
  2. Map Your Route — Use Great Circle Mapper (gcmap.com) to visualize your route and calculate distances. Ensure you follow all routing rules.
  3. Check Award Availability — Use tools like United.com, Aeroplan.com, or ExpertFlyer to search for business class award space on each segment. Look for "Saver" or "Standard" award space.
  4. Build Your Itinerary — Write down each flight with dates, flight numbers, and confirmation that award space exists. Have backup options ready.
  5. Transfer Points — Move points from credit card programs to your chosen airline. Allow 1-7 days for transfers (instant for some partners).
  6. Call to Book — For ANA and most programs, you'll need to call. Have your itinerary ready and be prepared to spell out city names and flight numbers.
  7. Hold vs. Ticket — Some programs allow holds (ANA: 48-96 hours). If you can't hold, ensure award space is still available before transferring points.
  8. Pay Taxes — Complete the booking by paying taxes and fees with a credit card that earns bonus points on travel.

Pro Tips for Booking Success

  • Book 11 months out: Award availability opens ~330 days in advance. The best business class seats go fast.
  • Be flexible: Have 2-3 date options for each segment. Tuesday/Wednesday departures often have better availability.
  • Use positioning flights: Book cheap cash tickets to get to your RTW starting point if needed.
  • Check partner availability: ANA can book any Star Alliance partner. Search United.com first, then call ANA.
  • Consider mixed cabin: If business isn't available on one segment, some programs allow economy on that leg at a reduced price.

Common RTW Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced travelers make these mistakes:

  • Not checking visa requirements: Some countries require visas in advance. Research each destination.
  • Ignoring fuel surcharges: Programs like ANA pass on carrier-imposed surcharges. Avoid Lufthansa and Austrian when possible.
  • Booking too tight: Leave buffer days between segments. Delays happen.
  • Forgetting about positioning: Factor in cost/time to reach your RTW departure city.
  • Not maximizing stopovers: You're allowed multiple stops — use them! Don't just pass through.
  • Transferring points too early: Only transfer when you have confirmed award space. Transfers are irreversible.

The Bottom Line

Round-the-World tickets represent one of the best values in the points and miles world. For 125,000 to 240,000 miles, you can visit 5-8 countries across multiple continents in business class — an experience that would cost $10,000 to $25,000 in cash.

The key is choosing the right program for your needs:

  • Maximum value: ANA Mileage Club
  • Maximum flexibility: Air Canada Aeroplan
  • Premium experience: Singapore KrisFlyer
  • Oneworld access: Cathay Pacific Asia Miles

Start by earning transferable points (Chase Ultimate Rewards, Amex Membership Rewards, Capital One Miles) so you have options when booking time comes. Research your dream route, check award availability 11 months out, and don't be afraid to call the airline to piece together your perfect around-the-world adventure.

The world is waiting — and with the right strategy, it's more accessible than you ever imagined.
