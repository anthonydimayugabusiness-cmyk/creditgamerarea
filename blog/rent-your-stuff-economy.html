The Rent Your Stuff Economy: How to Make $1,000+/Month Renting Your Car, Gear & Space

Turn your idle assets into passive income streams with platforms like Turo, Fat Llama, Neighbor, and RVshare

Your car sits in the driveway 95% of the time. Your camera gear collects dust between projects. That spare bedroom? It's basically a storage unit for things you never use. Meanwhile, people in your area are actively searching to rent exactly what you own.

Welcome to the "Rent Your Stuff" economy — a booming marketplace where everyday people are turning idle assets into serious passive income. We're talking $500 to $3,000+ per month without quitting your day job.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll break down the top platforms, real earnings data, insurance considerations, tax implications, and proven strategies from top earners. By the end, you'll know exactly how to monetize what you already own.

Platform Deep Dive: Where the Money Is

🚗 Turo — The Airbnb for Cars

💰 Average: $906/month | Top Hosts: $3,500+/month

What it is: Turo is the world's largest car sharing marketplace, connecting vehicle owners with renters who need temporary transportation. Think Airbnb, but for your car.

How much you can make:

  • Average car: $500-$1,200/month depending on location and vehicle type
  • Luxury vehicles: $1,500-$3,500/month (Tesla Model 3, BMW, Mercedes)
  • Peak season: Hosts can earn $3,500+ during summer months
  • Multiple cars: Fleet operators report $200+ profit per car monthly

Best vehicles to list:

  • Tesla Model 3/Y (high demand, premium rates)
  • Jeep Wranglers (perfect for vacation destinations)
  • Pickup trucks (constant demand from movers/DIYers)
  • Minivans (family vacation rentals)
  • Convertibles (weekend/seasonal premium)

Getting started:

  1. Create a host account at turo.com
  2. Upload photos of your vehicle (professional shots help)
  3. Set your availability and pricing (Turo's calculator helps)
  4. Choose a protection plan (liability coverage up to $750K)
  5. Respond quickly to booking requests (under 1 hour is ideal)

💡 Pro Tip from Top Earners

Hosts who offer delivery to airports and hotels earn 40% more than those with pickup-only options. Consider offering contactless key exchange through Turo's lockbox partners.

📷 Fat Llama — Rent Your Gear

💰 Average: £40-200/month | Power Users: £500+/month

What it is: Fat Llama (now expanding globally as a peer-to-peer rental marketplace) lets you rent out cameras, drones, audio equipment, tools, camping gear, and virtually anything else.

How much you can make:

  • Camera bodies: $30-80/day (Sony A7 series, Canon R5)
  • Professional lenses: $20-50/day (70-200mm, wide angles)
  • Drones: $40-100/day (DJI Mavic, Air series)
  • Power tools: $15-40/day (pressure washers, tile cutters)
  • Audio equipment: $25-60/day (microphones, mixers)

Best items to rent:

  • Professional camera equipment (high demand from content creators)
  • Drones (everyone wants aerial footage, few want to buy)
  • Specialized tools (tile cutters, floor sanders — buy once, rent many)
  • Camping gear (tents, kayaks for weekend warriors)
  • Gaming equipment (VR headsets, high-end PCs)

Getting started:

  1. Sign up at fatllama.com
  2. List your items with detailed descriptions and quality photos
  3. Set competitive daily rates (check similar listings)
  4. Verify your ID and connect a payout method
  5. Fat Llama takes 25% commission; insurance is included

💡 Pro Tip from Gear Renters

Bundle related items (camera + lens + tripod) at a slight discount. It increases your average order value and helps renters get everything they need in one place.

📦 Neighbor — The Airbnb for Storage

💰 Average: $100-300/month | Large Spaces: $500+/month

What it is: Neighbor connects people who need storage space with hosts who have extra room — garages, driveways, spare bedrooms, basements, even parking spots.

How much you can make:

  • Spare bedroom: $100-250/month
  • Garage space: $150-400/month
  • Driveway/parking: $50-200/month (higher in cities)
  • RV/boat parking: $100-300/month
  • Large warehouse space: $500-2,000+/month

Best spaces to list:

  • Garages in urban areas (high demand, limited supply)
  • Driveway parking near airports (travelers need long-term parking)
  • Climate-controlled basements (furniture, document storage)
  • Large sheds or barns (RVs, classic cars, equipment)
  • Spare closets in apartment buildings (urban storage crunch)

Getting started:

  1. Create a host account at neighbor.com
  2. Measure and photograph your space accurately
  3. Set your price (Neighbor shows comparable rates in your area)
  4. Specify access hours and any restrictions
  5. Neighbor provides $1M host liability protection

💡 Pro Tip from Storage Hosts

Hosts who respond to inquiries within 30 minutes get 3x more bookings. Set up notifications and be ready to answer questions about security, access, and dimensions quickly.

🚐 RVshare — Rent Your RV

💰 Average: $1,000-2,000/month | Peak Season: $3,000+/month

What it is: RVshare is the largest peer-to-peer RV rental marketplace. RV owners list their vehicles when not in use, and travelers rent them for road trips and camping adventures.

How much you can make:

  • Travel trailers: $800-1,500/month (6-10 rentals typical)
  • Class C motorhomes: $2,000-4,000/month
  • Class A motorhomes: $3,000-6,000/month
  • Pop-up campers: $500-1,000/month
  • Annual potential: $30,000-40,000 for active listings

Getting started:

  1. List your RV at rvshare.com with detailed photos
  2. Set nightly rates based on your RV type and seasonality
  3. Choose your delivery options (stationary vs. delivered)
  4. RVshare provides up to $1M liability coverage
  5. Consider offering delivery for premium rates ($1.35-1.85/mile)

💡 Pro Tip from RV Hosts

RVs that include delivery and setup services rent 2x more frequently. Many renters are first-timers who don't want to tow. Charge a delivery fee plus mileage for this premium service.

🚙 Getaround — Instant Car Sharing

💰 Average: $400-800/month | Premium Cars: $1,200+/month

What it is: Getaround offers contactless, instant car sharing using connected car technology. Renters unlock cars through the app — no key exchange needed.

How much you can make:

  • Economy cars: $300-600/month
  • Mid-size sedans: $500-900/month
  • Luxury vehicles: $1,000-2,000/month
  • Hourly rates: $8-20/hour depending on vehicle

Key differences from Turo:

  • Getaround Connect device enables instant, contactless rentals
  • Shorter rental periods (hours vs. days) = higher turnover
  • More urban-focused (great for city cars)
  • Getaround takes 40% commission vs. Turo's 15-40% sliding scale

Getting started:

  1. Apply at getaround.com (vehicle must be 2008 or newer)
  2. Install the Getaround Connect device ($99 one-time)
  3. Set your car's availability calendar
  4. Getaround handles all renter verification and insurance

Before you list anything, understand how you're protected. Each platform handles insurance differently:

Fat Llama Insurance included

Key Insurance Considerations

1. Your Personal Insurance
Most personal auto policies have exclusions for commercial use. Contact your insurer before listing your car. Some companies offer "ride-share endorsements" that cover gaps.

2. Platform vs. Personal Coverage
Platform insurance typically covers incidents during the rental period. Damage that occurs while the vehicle is in your possession usually falls to your personal policy.

3. Deductibles
Turo's protection plans have deductibles ranging from $0 to $2,500. The higher your deductible, the lower your commission fee to Turo. Choose based on your risk tolerance.

4. Storage Liability
Neighbor's $1M protection covers property damage and liability, but not the renter's stored items. Renters should carry their own insurance for their belongings.

⚠️ Important: Read the Fine Print

Insurance coverage varies by state and can change. Always review the current terms of service before listing. When in doubt, consult with an insurance professional about your specific situation.

Tax Implications: What the IRS Wants

Rental income is taxable income — but the good news is you can deduct legitimate business expenses. Here's what you need to know:

Reporting Requirements

  • 1099 Forms: Platforms issue 1099-K or 1099-MISC if you earn over $600/year
  • Self-Employment Tax: Income is subject to income tax plus self-employment tax (15.3%)
  • Quarterly Payments: If you expect to owe $1,000+ in taxes, pay quarterly estimated taxes

Deductible Expenses

  • Vehicle expenses: Mileage, maintenance, cleaning, depreciation
  • Platform fees: The commission taken by Turo, Getaround, etc.
  • Insurance: Additional coverage purchased for rentals
  • Equipment: Cameras, lockboxes, cleaning supplies
  • Home office: If you dedicate space to managing rentals
  • Professional services: Tax prep, legal advice, photography

💡 Tax Pro Tip

Use dedicated bank accounts and tracking apps like Hurdlr or Everlance to automatically track income and expenses. Good records can save you thousands at tax time and protect you in case of an audit.

Tips for Maximizing Bookings and Ratings

Your rating is your livelihood on these platforms. Here's how top hosts maintain 5-star status:

🚗 For Car Rentals

  • Photos matter: Professional photos get 3x more bookings
  • Cleanliness is non-negotiable: Detail between every rental
  • Respond fast: Under 1 hour response time is the standard
  • Be flexible: Offer airport delivery and early/late pickup when possible
  • Communicate proactively: Send pickup instructions before the rental starts
  • Document everything: Photos of fuel level, mileage, condition at pickup and return

📷 For Gear Rentals

  • Create checklists: Include everything the renter receives (cables, batteries, cases)
  • Test before sending: Verify everything works before each rental
  • Include tutorials: Link to YouTube videos for complex equipment
  • Offer insurance: Make it clear what's covered and what's not
  • Package securely: Use proper cases to prevent shipping damage

📦 For Storage Hosts

  • Be specific: Exact dimensions, ceiling height, access hours
  • Security features: Mention cameras, locks, climate control
  • Easy access: Clear pathways, good lighting, convenient hours
  • Responsive communication: Renters may need emergency access

Getting Started: Your 30-Day Action Plan

🚀 Week 1: Inventory & Research

  1. Audit your assets: What do you own that sits unused? Car, gear, space?
  2. Research your market: Check what's already listed in your area and at what prices
  3. Check your insurance: Call your provider about coverage gaps
  4. Choose your platform: Start with one — don't spread yourself thin

📸 Week 2: Listing Creation

  1. Photograph everything: Natural light, multiple angles, show condition
  2. Write detailed descriptions: Include specs, features, restrictions
  3. Set competitive pricing: Start 10-15% below market to get initial bookings
  4. Create templates: Pre-write responses to common questions

🎯 Week 3: Launch & Optimize

  1. Go live: Publish your listings and share on social media
  2. Enable instant book: If available, it significantly increases bookings
  3. Set notifications: Respond to inquiries within minutes, not hours
  4. Track everything: Start logging income and expenses from day one

📈 Week 4: Scale & Systematize

  1. Review your data: What's working? What's not?
  2. Adjust pricing: Raise rates if you're fully booked; lower if you're empty
  3. Ask for reviews: Follow up with renters for feedback and ratings
  4. Plan expansion: Ready for a second asset? Start the process again

The Bottom Line

The "Rent Your Stuff" economy represents one of the most accessible forms of passive income available today. You don't need startup capital beyond what you already own. You don't need special skills or certifications. You just need assets sitting idle and the willingness to share them.

Start small. List one thing. Learn the platform. Build your ratings. Then scale.

The top earners in this space aren't doing anything magical — they're just consistent. They respond quickly, maintain high standards, and treat their rentals like the businesses they are.

Your car, your gear, your space — they're not just expenses. They're assets waiting to generate income. The only question is: when will you start?
