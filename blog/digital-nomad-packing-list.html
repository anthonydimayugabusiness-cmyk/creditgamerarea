The Digital Nomad Packing List: 15 Items That Changed How I Travel

After 3 years of full-time travel, these 15 items are always in my bag. From tech gadgets to comfort essentials, here's what actually matters on the road.

February 25, 2026 • 10 min read

Three years ago, I packed everything I owned into a 40L backpack and bought a one-way ticket to Lisbon. Since then, I've worked from cafes in Tokyo, beaches in Bali, and coworking spaces in Mexico City.

I've also made every packing mistake possible. I brought things I never used. I forgot things I desperately needed. I learned the hard way what actually matters when your entire life fits in a bag.

Here's what's always in my pack now—tested across 20+ countries and countless airports.

Tech Essentials

1 Noise-Canceling Headphones

Not earbuds. Full over-ear headphones. The difference between arriving rested or ruined on a long flight is worth every ounce of extra weight.

Why it matters: They create a bubble of focus anywhere—crying babies on planes, loud cafes, noisy hostels. My productivity (and sanity) depends on them.

2 Universal Travel Adapter with USB-C PD

The cheap adapters break. The good ones last years and charge your laptop, phone, and headphones simultaneously.

Why it matters: One outlet in a hostel room becomes four. I've made friends by sharing charging ports at airports.

3 Portable Power Bank (20,000mAh+)

Your phone is your map, translator, camera, and boarding pass. A dead phone in a foreign city is a special kind of stress.

Why it matters: I've navigated Tokyo at midnight, found my Airbnb in Lisbon's winding streets, and translated emergency pharmacy visits—all because I had power.

4 E-Reader (Kindle or Similar)

Physical books are heavy. Phone screens strain your eyes. An e-reader holds a library and weighs less than one paperback.

Why it matters:>/strong> Long flights, beach days, and insomnia in new time zones all become opportunities instead of ordeals.

Comfort & Sleep

5 Merino Wool Buff/Neck Gaiter

It's a scarf, eye mask, headband, beanie, and face covering. Merino wool doesn't smell, dries fast, and regulates temperature.

Why it matters: I've used it as a pillowcase on sketchy hostel beds, a sun shield in the Sahara, and a mask during dust storms.

6 Inflatable Travel Pillow (The Good Kind)

Not the U-shaped ones that push your head forward. The inflatable ones that support your neck properly.

Why it matters: Arriving functional after an overnight flight is the difference between losing a day or hitting the ground running.

7 Packing Cubes (Compression Type)

They don't just organize—they compress. A week's clothes becomes three days' worth of space.

Why it matters: Living out of a bag means chaos without organization. Cubes let me find anything in 10 seconds without unpacking everything.

Health & Wellness

8 Collapsible Water Bottle

Empty it through security, fill it on the other side. Saves money, reduces plastic, and keeps you hydrated on long travel days.

Why it matters: Dehydration causes jet lag, headaches, and bad decisions. Having water always available is non-negotiable.

9 Basic First Aid Kit (Customized)

Not the pre-made ones. Build your own: your preferred pain reliever, anti-diarrheal, allergy meds, blister pads, and any prescriptions.

Why it matters: Finding familiar medication in a foreign pharmacy at 2 AM with food poisoning is an adventure you don't want.

10 Microfiber Towel

Dries in hours, not days. Takes up 1/4 the space of a regular towel. Essential for hostels, beaches, and unexpected situations.

Why it matters: Some hostels don't provide towels. Some beaches charge for them. Being self-sufficient means never being stuck.

Productivity & Work

11 Laptop Stand (Portable)

Working hunched over a laptop destroys your neck and back. A portable stand elevates your screen to eye level.

Why it matters: I can work from anywhere for hours without pain. Coffee shops, airports, apartments—it all becomes ergonomic.

12 Notebook and Pen (Yes, Really)

Digital notes are searchable. Paper notes are memorable. I use both. The notebook is for ideas, sketches, and journaling.

Why it matters: Some of my best ideas came from journaling in a cafe in Prague. The act of writing by hand unlocks different thinking.

The Intangibles

13 A Good Daypack (Packable)

Your main bag stays at the Airbnb. Your daypack goes everywhere. It should pack into itself when not in use.

Why it matters: Grocery runs, beach days, coworking sessions—having a light bag for daily carry changes everything.

14 Solid Toiletries (Shampoo/Conditioner Bars)

No liquid limits. No spills. Lasts longer than bottles. Better for the environment. Win-win-win-win.

Why it matters: TSA becomes a non-issue. My bag never has shampoo explosions. And I'm not throwing away plastic bottles every month.

15 A Physical Copy of Important Info

Passport copy. Emergency contacts. Backup credit card numbers. Addresses. Written on actual paper, stored separately from your passport.

Why it matters: Phones die. Wallets get stolen. Having physical backups has saved me twice when everything else failed.

What I DON'T Pack Anymore

Just as important as what to bring is what to leave behind:

  • "Just in case" clothes — You can buy anything you actually need
  • Multiple pairs of shoes — One comfortable pair, one dressy pair. That's it.
  • Books (physical) — Love them, but the weight isn't worth it
  • Travel pillow (the bad kind) — Those U-shaped inflatable ones are useless
  • Excess tech — You don't need a tablet, laptop, e-reader, AND a Switch. Choose two.

The Philosophy

The goal isn't to pack light for the sake of it. The goal is to remove friction from travel. Every item should earn its place by solving a real problem you've actually had.

Start with everything. Pay attention to what you use. Remove what you don't. Repeat.

After three years, this is where I landed. Your list will be different—but the process is the same.
