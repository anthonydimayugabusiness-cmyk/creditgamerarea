Credit Card Rewards Deep Dive

The Ultimate Amex Membership Rewards Guide: How to Get 2+ Cents Per Point

Master the art of transferring points to ANA, Aeroplan, and Avios. Learn the sweet spots that can turn 100,000 points into $3,000+ of travel value.

📅 Feb 23, 2026 ⏱️ 15 min read 💎 Points & Miles Strategy

Let me tell you about the flight that changed how I think about credit card points.

Last year, I flew Lufthansa First Class from Frankfurt to Tokyo — a ticket that costs $8,000+ in cash. I paid just 85,000 Amex Membership Rewards points transferred to ANA Mileage Club. That's nearly 10 cents per point in value.

But here's the thing: most people redeem their Amex points for 0.7-1 cent each through the Amex travel portal or (worse) for gift cards. They're leaving thousands of dollars on the table.

In this comprehensive guide, I'll show you exactly how to extract maximum value from your Amex Membership Rewards points. We'll cover the best transfer partners, the sweetest redemption sweet spots, card comparisons, and how Amex stacks up against Chase Ultimate Rewards.

What Are Amex Membership Rewards Points?

Amex Membership Rewards (MR) is American Express's flexible points currency. Unlike airline-specific miles or hotel points, MR points can be transferred to 20+ airline and hotel partners, giving you incredible flexibility.

The key advantage? Transfer partners often unlock outsized value — especially for premium cabin international flights that would cost thousands in cash.

The Holy Trinity: Best Amex Transfer Partners

While Amex has many transfer partners, three stand head and shoulders above the rest for U.S.-based travelers: ANA Mileage Club, Air Canada Aeroplan, and British Airways Avios. Master these three, and you'll unlock 90% of the best redemptions.

1. ANA Mileage Club: The First Class Secret Weapon

All Nippon Airways (ANA) is a Star Alliance member, which means you can use ANA miles to book flights on United, Lufthansa, Swiss, Singapore Airlines, and more. But the real magic is ANA's own premium cabins.

Why ANA is special:

  • Round-trip award chart: ANA prices awards as round-trips, which sounds limiting but actually creates incredible value
  • Low mileage requirements: Business class to Europe starts at just 88,000 miles round-trip
  • First class sweet spot: 150,000-165,000 miles for round-trip first class to Japan (vs. 220,000+ with other programs)
  • Stopover allowed: Free stopover on round-trip awards

🎯 ANA Sweet Spot: The "Round the World" Trick

ANA's round-trip pricing means you can fly NYC → Tokyo (stopover) → Bangkok → NYC for the same miles as a simple round-trip to Tokyo. The key is routing through ANA's hub in Tokyo. This effectively gives you two destinations for the price of one.

Best ANA Redemptions:

  • USA to Japan Business: 85,000-95,000 miles round-trip
  • USA to Japan First Class: 150,000-165,000 miles round-trip
  • USA to Europe Business: 88,000 miles round-trip (on Star Alliance partners)
  • USA to Southeast Asia Business: 136,000 miles round-trip

The catch: ANA requires you to book round-trip, and availability can be scarce — especially for first class. You need to be flexible with dates and book 330 days out when seats are released.

2. Air Canada Aeroplan: The Flexible Workhorse

Aeroplan underwent a massive overhaul in 2020 and emerged as one of the most versatile Star Alliance programs. If ANA is a sniper rifle (precise but limited), Aeroplan is a Swiss Army knife.

Why Aeroplan shines:

  • One-way awards: Book just the direction you need
  • No fuel surcharges: On most partners (huge savings on Lufthansa and Austrian)
  • Stopovers for 5,000 miles: Add a stopover to any one-way award for just 5,000 extra miles
  • Family pooling: Pool points with up to 8 family members
  • Points + Cash: Flexible redemption options

Best Aeroplan Redemptions:

  • USA to Europe Business: 60,000-70,000 miles one-way
  • USA to Japan Business: 55,000-75,000 miles one-way
  • Short-haul North America: 6,000-10,000 miles (United domestic flights)
  • Stopover in Europe: Fly NYC → Paris (stopover) → Rome for 65,000 miles + $50 CAD

Pro tip: Aeroplan is often the best way to book Lufthansa First Class because they don't pass on fuel surcharges. A one-way first class ticket from USA to Europe costs around 90,000-100,000 miles plus minimal taxes.

3. British Airways Avios: The Short-Haul Specialist

British Airways Executive Club uses "Avios" as its currency, and while long-haul flights on BA carry hefty fuel surcharges, the program excels at short-haul redemptions — especially within Europe, Asia, and Australia.

Why Avios matters:

  • Distance-based award chart: Short flights are incredibly cheap
  • OneWorld alliance: Redeem on American Airlines, Qantas, Cathay Pacific, Japan Airlines
  • No close-in booking fees: Unlike American Airlines
  • Combine with Qatar: Qatar Airways adopted Avios, expanding your options

🎯 Avios Sweet Spot: American Airlines Domestic

Flights under 650 miles cost just 7,500 Avios. That covers routes like NYC-Boston, Miami-Orlando, or LA-San Francisco. Compare that to American's dynamic pricing, which often charges 20,000+ miles for the same flights.

Best Avios Redemptions:

  • Short domestic flights on AA: 7,500-11,000 Avios
  • West Coast to Hawaii on Alaska/AA: 13,000-20,000 Avios
  • Intra-Europe on BA/Iberia: 4,500-8,000 Avios (plus minimal taxes)
  • East Coast to Dublin on Aer Lingus: 13,000 Avios off-peak
  • Australia domestic on Qantas: 6,000-11,000 Avios

Other Notable Transfer Partners

While the "Big Three" should be your go-tos, these partners have specific use cases:

Singapore Airlines KrisFlyer

Singapore Airlines only releases premium cabin awards (Suites, First, Business) to its own program. If you want to experience the famous Singapore Suites with the double bed, you need KrisFlyer miles. USA to Singapore in Suites costs around 132,000-148,000 miles one-way.

Emirates Skywards

Want to fly Emirates First Class with the onboard shower and bar? You'll need Skywards miles. Amex transfers 1:1, and first class from USA to Dubai runs 135,000-178,000 miles one-way depending on the route.

Virgin Atlantic Flying Club

Excellent for ANA flights (yes, really!) — Virgin prices ANA awards lower than ANA itself in some cases. Also great for Delta One business class to Europe at 50,000 miles one-way (when available).

Hilton Honors

Amex transfers 1:2 to Hilton, which sounds great but usually isn't. Only transfer when you have a specific high-value redemption in mind, like the Conrad Maldives at 120,000 points/night (60,000 MR points).

How to Get 2+ Cents Per Point (Consistently)

The travel blogosphere loves to claim you can get "2+ cents per point" with strategic redemptions. But how do you actually do it consistently? Here's the playbook:

1. Target Premium Cabins on Long-Haul Flights

Economy redemptions rarely exceed 1.5 cents per point. The value comes from business and first class, where cash prices are astronomical but points prices are only moderately higher than economy.

💎 Real-World Value Examples

ANA First Class NYC → Tokyo (Round-trip) ~10¢/pt
Lufthansa First Class USA → Europe (One-way) ~8¢/pt
Aeroplan Business USA → Japan (One-way) ~4¢/pt
Avios Short-haul Domestic ~2¢/pt
Amex Travel Portal (Baseline) 1¢/pt

2. Book During Peak Cash Pricing

Points prices are typically fixed (or have limited ranges), while cash prices fluctuate wildly. A business class ticket to Europe might cost $2,000 in January but $6,000 in July. The points price stays roughly the same — meaning your cent-per-point value triples in summer.

3. Use Transfer Bonuses

Amex frequently offers transfer bonuses — typically 20-40% extra when transferring to specific partners. A 30% bonus to Avios effectively means you're getting 1.3x the value. Stack this with sweet spot redemptions for massive returns.

4. Avoid Fuel Surcharges

Some programs (like British Airways for long-haul) pass on carrier-imposed surcharges that can exceed $1,000 on premium cabins. Programs like Aeroplan and United don't, saving you serious cash.

Amex Card Comparison: Business Platinum vs Gold vs Green

Which Amex card should you get? It depends on your spending patterns and travel goals. Here's the breakdown:

American Express Business Platinum

Annual Fee: $695

Welcome Bonus: 150,000+ points (varies by offer)

Key Benefits:

  • 5x points on flights and prepaid hotels via Amex Travel
  • 1.5x points on eligible purchases in key business categories and on purchases of $5,000 or more (up to $2 million per year)
  • Airport lounge access: Centurion Lounges, Priority Pass, Delta Sky Clubs (when flying Delta)
  • $200 airline fee credit
  • $200 hotel credit (Fine Hotels + Resorts or The Hotel Collection)
  • $189 CLEAR credit
  • 35% points rebate when booking first/business class via Amex Pay with Points (up to 1 million points back per year)
  • Global Entry/TSA PreCheck credit
  • Gold status with Hilton and Marriott

Best for: Frequent business travelers who can utilize the lounge network and will book flights through Amex Travel. The 35% rebate effectively gives you 1.54 cents per point on premium cabin bookings — competitive with many transfer options.

American Express Gold Card

Annual Fee: $325

Welcome Bonus: 60,000+ points

Key Benefits:

  • 4x points at restaurants worldwide (including takeout and delivery in the U.S.)
  • 4x points at U.S. supermarkets (up to $25,000 per year, then 1x)
  • 3x points on flights booked directly with airlines or via Amex Travel
  • $120 dining credit ($10/month at select restaurants)
  • $120 Uber Cash ($10/month)
  • No foreign transaction fees

Best for: Foodies and everyday spenders. The 4x categories cover most people's largest expenses after rent/mortgage. If you spend $1,000/month on dining and groceries, you're earning 48,000 points annually just from those categories.

American Express Green Card

Annual Fee: $150

Welcome Bonus: 40,000+ points

Key Benefits:

  • 3x points on travel (flights, hotels, transit, rideshares, tours)
  • 3x points at restaurants worldwide
  • 3x points on transit (subways, buses, trains, ferries)
  • $189 CLEAR credit
  • $100 LoungeBuddy credit
  • No foreign transaction fees

Best for: Budget-conscious travelers who want a lower annual fee but still earn bonus points on travel and dining. The Green Card is often overlooked but offers solid value for the price.

🎯 My Recommendation: The Two-Card Strategy

Most people should start with the Gold Card for the 4x dining and grocery multipliers. Add the Business Platinum if you travel frequently enough to justify the lounge access and 35% rebate. Use the Gold for everyday spending, the Platinum for flights and hotel bookings.

Amex vs Chase: Which Points Program Wins?

The eternal debate: Membership Rewards or Ultimate Rewards? Here's how they stack up:

Transfer Partners

Winner: Tie (with caveats)

Both programs have excellent transfer partners, but they excel in different regions:

  • Europe: Amex wins with ANA and Aeroplan for Star Alliance access
  • Asia: Amex wins with ANA, Singapore, and Cathay Pacific
  • Domestic USA: Chase wins with Southwest and United
  • Hyatt: Chase wins — Hyatt is a Chase exclusive and offers exceptional value

Earning Rates

Winner: Chase (for most people)

The Chase trifecta (Sapphire Reserve/Preferred + Freedom Flex + Freedom Unlimited) offers:

  • 3x on dining and travel (Reserve) or 2x (Preferred)
  • 5x rotating categories (Freedom Flex)
  • 1.5x on everything else (Freedom Unlimited)

Amex's 4x on dining and groceries (Gold Card) is excellent, but Chase's 5x rotating categories and 1.5x base rate on the Freedom Unlimited are hard to beat for diversified spending.

Ease of Redemption

Winner: Chase

Chase's travel portal offers 1.25-1.5 cents per point (depending on your card), making it easy to get solid value without hunting for award availability. Amex's portal is clunkier and only offers 1 cent per point (unless you have the Business Platinum's 35% rebate).

Premium Redemptions

Winner: Amex

For aspirational travel — first class to Asia, Lufthansa First to Europe, Emirates Suites — Amex's transfer partners are unmatched. ANA, Singapore, and Emirates are Amex exclusives in the U.S. transferable points space.

Annual Fees

Winner: Chase

Chase Sapphire Preferred ($95) and Reserve ($550) are generally easier to justify than Amex Platinum ($695) or Business Platinum ($695). The Chase ecosystem is also more accessible for those not ready to commit to high annual fees.

🎯 The Verdict: Which Should You Choose?

  1. Start with Chase if you're new to points. The Sapphire Preferred is the best beginner travel card, and the ecosystem is more forgiving.
  2. Add Amex once you're comfortable with transfer partners and want access to premium cabin sweet spots.
  3. Run both if you can justify the annual fees. They complement each other perfectly — Chase for domestic/Hyatt, Amex for international premium cabins.

Action Plan: Your First 100,000 Points

Just got your first Amex card and earned the welcome bonus? Here's how to turn those points into memorable travel:

Option 1: The "Once in a Lifetime" Trip (ANA First Class)

  • Transfer 165,000 MR points to ANA (you'll need to earn a bit more)
  • Book round-trip first class from USA to Tokyo
  • Add a stopover in Tokyo for free
  • Value: $15,000+ in cash
  • Best for: Honeymoons, milestone birthdays, bucket list travel

Option 2: The European Adventure (Aeroplan)

  • Transfer 60,000-70,000 MR points to Aeroplan
  • Book one-way business class to Europe
  • Add a stopover for 5,000 more miles
  • Pay minimal taxes (Aeroplan doesn't pass on fuel surcharges)
  • Value: $3,000-4,000 in cash
  • Best for: Couples traveling together, multi-city European trips

Option 3: The Domestic Hacker (Avios)

  • Transfer with a 20-40% bonus when available
  • Book 10-13 short-haul domestic flights at 7,500-11,000 Avios each
  • Perfect for visiting family, weekend getaways
  • Value: $2,000-3,000 in cash
  • Best for: Frequent domestic travelers, families

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Don't let these errors sabotage your points strategy:

1. Transferring Points Speculatively
Only transfer when you have a specific booking in mind. Transfers are irreversible, and programs can devalue without warning.

2. Ignoring Taxes and Fees
British Airways and Air France-KLM pass on hefty fuel surcharges. A "free" ticket might cost $500+ in taxes. Always check the total cost before transferring.

3. Booking Economy
Economy redemptions rarely beat 1.5 cents per point. Save your points for business and first class where the value multiplies.

4. Missing Transfer Bonuses
Amex runs 20-40% transfer bonuses several times per year. Wait for these if your travel dates are flexible.

5. Not Having a Backup Plan
Award availability changes daily. Have 2-3 options for your trip in case your first choice disappears.

The Bottom Line

Amex Membership Rewards is the most powerful points currency for aspirational travel. While Chase Ultimate Rewards offers simplicity and better domestic options, Amex unlocks experiences that are simply unattainable with cash — first class suites, onboard showers, lie-flat beds to anywhere in the world.

The key is knowing which transfer partner to use for each trip:

  • ANA for round-trip premium cabin awards to Asia
  • Aeroplan for flexible one-way bookings and stopovers
  • Avios for short-haul flights and domestic hops

Master these three programs, and you'll consistently extract 2-10 cents per point in value — turning a 100,000-point welcome bonus into $2,000-$10,000 worth of travel.

Your first class seat is waiting. The only question is: where will you go?
